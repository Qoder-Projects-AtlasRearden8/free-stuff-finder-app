The human part

Free is a promise.
We spell out the fine print.

The internet has plenty of “free” lists. This one is meant to respect your time: clear limits, honest caveats, and a date saying when we last looked.

  1. 01

    Limits, not marketing.

    Every card explains what the free tier includes and where it stops. If a plan has a message cap, a fee, or a hardware catch, we say so.

  2. 02

    A date you can check.

    “Last verified” is shown on every listing. Free offers drift, so each date is a snapshot—not a forever promise. Recheck before you rely on it.

  3. 03

    No pay-to-play.

    No affiliate links and no paid placements. A listing being here is not an endorsement of every feature, policy, or upgrade pitch.

A note about assistance programs. Eligibility, hours, and funding can change quickly. This directory is for education and referrals only—not legal, medical, tax, or financial advice. Check details directly with the organization.

Add a freebie

Small pull request. Big help.

Freebie Finder stays useful because people check real offers and describe the catch. Here is the whole process:

  1. 1

    Find a genuinely free resource.

    It needs a free tier, free course, or real assistance program—not a trial that expires in 24 hours.

  2. 2

    Fork the repository and edit one JSON file.

    Add an entry to data/resources.json. Keep the description warm and plain, and write limits without marketing spin.

  3. 3

    Check it, test it, then open a PR.

    Run npm test, preview with npm start, and include the source you used in your pull request.

No affiliate links, referral codes, API keys, personal data, or scraped marketing copy. A maintainer reviews every entry before it lands.
